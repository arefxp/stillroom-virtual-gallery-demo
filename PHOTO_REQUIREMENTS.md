# Photos needed for your virtual gallery tour

**Best option: 360° photos of the exhibition.** Please take one at the entrance and one at each place where a visitor should be able to “stand.” We will connect those spots with on-screen arrows. Visitors can look all around from each spot, then move to the next one.

## Capture checklist

- Use a 360° camera or phone panorama setup that exports a **full 360° equirectangular image** (usually a 2:1 image). A normal wide photo is not the same thing.
- Place the camera at roughly eye height. Keep it level and in the same kind of position for each shot.
- Shoot at every doorway, turn, and important artwork grouping. Each next position should be easy to recognize from the previous one.
- Keep the lighting and exhibition layout the same throughout the shoot. Remove people and moving objects where possible.
- Send the **original full-resolution files**. Please avoid screenshots, social-media downloads, heavy compression, or files with logos and text added.
- Send a separate, straight-on, high-resolution photo of **each artwork** for its close-up view, plus its title, artist name, year, and any description you want shown.
- A quick phone video walking through the rooms, or even a hand-drawn floor sketch, helps us place the arrows. No CAD drawing is required for this style of tour.
- Confirm that you have permission to show the gallery and every artwork online.

## If you only have regular photos

We can still make a linked-view tour, but visitors will jump between photographed angles. They will not be able to turn freely through a full 360° view. A smooth 3D walk-through needs a much denser, overlapping photo or video capture and is a separate project.

## Simple starting set

For a small one-room exhibition, start with 4–8 well-spaced 360° positions plus one close-up file for each artwork. The exact count depends on the room and where the walls or displays block the view.
