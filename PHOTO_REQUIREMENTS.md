# Photos needed for your virtual gallery tour

**Best option: 360° photos of the exhibition, taken every 1.5–2 meters.** Each photo becomes a spot where a visitor can “stand” and look all around. We connect neighbouring spots with arrows on the floor, so visitors can step forward, back, or sideways through each room, like Google Street View. The closer together the photos are, the more it feels like walking.

## Capture checklist

- Use a 360° camera or phone panorama setup that exports a **full 360° equirectangular image** (usually a 2:1 image). A normal wide photo is not the same thing.
- Place the camera at roughly eye height. Keep it level and in the same kind of position for each shot.
- Walk the route a visitor would take and shoot a photo every 1.5–2 meters (about two to three normal steps). Use a slightly tighter spacing in narrow spaces.
- Take one photo just before and one just after each doorway, so visitors can step through it smoothly.
- Also shoot in front of each important artwork or grouping. Each next position should be easy to recognize from the previous one.
- Keep the lighting and exhibition layout the same throughout the shoot. Remove people and moving objects where possible.
- Send the **original full-resolution files**. Please avoid screenshots, social-media downloads, heavy compression, or files with logos and text added.
- Send a separate, straight-on, high-resolution photo of **each artwork** for its close-up view, plus its title, artist name, year, and any description you want shown.
- A quick phone video walking through the rooms, or even a hand-drawn floor sketch, helps us place the arrows. No CAD drawing is required for this style of tour.
- Confirm that you have permission to show the gallery and every artwork online.

## If you only have regular photos

We can still make a linked-view tour, but visitors will jump between photographed angles. They will not be able to turn freely through a full 360° view. A smooth 3D walk-through needs a much denser, overlapping photo or video capture and is a separate project.

## Simple starting set

Expect roughly 6–12 positions for a medium-sized room, plus one close-up file for each artwork. As a guide, a 10 × 8 m room shot on a 2 m grid needs about 10–12 positions. The exact count depends on the room and on where walls or displays block the view.

## Optional: free-roam 3D walkthrough

To let visitors move anywhere smoothly instead of stepping between spots, we can build a 3D model of the gallery from a slow walkthrough video (a Gaussian splat). It needs its own on-site capture session, and we quote it separately.
